Long

Origin · I wasn't looking for more news. I was looking for understanding.

For a year I searched for a better AI-native workflow. Then a friend introduced me to one person's method, and it changed my craft. The answer already existed. I had no way to find it.

You don't know what you don't know.

Strategy · The brief as a start.

  • Trust in news is lost. So make evidence inspectable. Every claim links to dated sources. Repeated reporting never counts as corroboration. Nothing is sponsored.
  • News landscape is fragmented. So one question, not many topics. You follow one inquiry. The agent connects scattered sources into one picture, and stops when nothing changed.
  • Wants signal. So reasoning, not popularity. Discovery comes from how other people connected the dots, not from what holds attention.

Method · Build the intelligence before the interface.

  • Socratic, not surveyed. I challenged my own hypothesis and strategy with AI. Interviews were deferred: a 10-hour trade-off.
  • Useful engine before beautiful. A working research engine came first. On my own year-long question, it found angles I'd missed.
  • Smallest input, most value. One inquiry that keeps evolving replaces endless new searches and repeated exposure.
  • AI for speed, me for taste. Logic first, then my design system and references carried it to high fidelity.

Trade-offs · Ten hours, spent on why it should exist.

  • Polished the first visit. The returning-user home can wait.
  • Built the engine. User interviews come next.
  • Daily updates, built but off. The demo proves first value.
  • Connectors, later. X & YouTube blocks automated access.

One question. Less noise. More understanding.

The problem · One story, dozens of headlines.

News moves on and you start over. Every visit, you rebuild what you knew and guess what's genuinely new.

One story in five framings overloads attention, and the conflicting takes create dissonance. People resolve it by tuning out, or by keeping the version that already agrees with them.

Trust · People trust AI news even less.

37% trust news most of the time. 20% trust news from AI chatbots. The brief asks AI to repair a trust problem. People trust AI news even less. (Reuters Institute, Digital News Report 2026)

The bet · Follow the question.

Don't give people more news. Help them uncover the story. Say what you're trying to understand. NexGen News cuts through the noise and connects millions of dots into a meta perspective made for you.

Saying a question is faster than typing it, so voice comes first. Typing is the fallback. Every update answers: what's new, why it matters to you, what you're missing, and the evidence.

Thinking · The search is interactive.

Sources gather as a living field of dots while your inquiry progresses. Tap a cluster to see what the agent is looking at as it scans the web.

Under the hood · A real engine, not a mockup.

Each inquiry fetches real public sources, checks every quote against the original text, and writes what it learns into an Obsidian vault. The next visit starts from that memory.

No guesswork. Designing on top of a working engine grounds every UX and product decision in what the system can actually do. The graph is the real vault after four inquiries: 180 notes, 243 links.

While it reads · The wait is the welcome.

The inquiry is real and takes several minutes. Instead of a spinner, you browse closely related files other people have already compiled. It's also a good moment to show, step by step, how the app works.

Inquiry ready · It lands. You choose when.

Community files show how others connected the dots around closely related questions, and may already deepen your understanding. Open your first inquiry when you're done exploring. Community files always wait at the end of your own.

Home · Home is your question.

No feed. Your question, your last update, and one button to check what changed.

I reached the 10-hour limit here. I focused on onboarding and a preview of the next visit, so the screens from this point are unpolished. I'd replace the "Check what changed" button with a different interaction pattern.

What changed · Only what's new.

Each story says how far its evidence goes. Mark something you already knew and it won't come back as news.

The vision: the engine searches for new reporting on your inquiry once a day, in the background, so something new arrives without effort. A native notification says when it's ready.

Memory · It remembers what you told it.

Your marks shape the next update, so nothing repeats. Each visit brings a fresh perspective on what you want to understand.

Next · Help people uncover the story.

Next, turn on the inquiry engine so searches run in real time. Then test it with real people. Or keep iterating until every tester loves it and asks for more.

How I'd measure it. The test that counts: does it surface a consequential connection you would have missed, with evidence you can inspect? Then run two bets side by side, a reliably fresh briefing or a surprising perspective, to see which people value. Measure feeling caught up and return visits, never minutes in the app.
